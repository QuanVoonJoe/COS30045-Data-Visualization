The Energy Rating Label gives an appliance a star rating between 1 and 10 stars (with 6 stars being the standard benchmark, and 7 to 10 for super-efficient appliances). It also lists the tested annual energy consumption in kilowatt-hours (kWh) so you can directly compare running costs.

Divide the appliance wattage by 1,000 to convert to kilowatts (e.g. 100W = 0.1 kW). Multiply that by daily running hours to find daily kilowatt-hours (kWh). Then multiply by your retailer's electricity tariff in cents/kWh (e.g. 31.5¢ = $0.315 AUD).

Panel technology, screen diagonal, and peak HDR brightness drastically alter power draw. Standard edge-lit LED screens use around 60W, while large 75" to 85" Mini-LED or high-luminance screens can exceed 220W during playback.
